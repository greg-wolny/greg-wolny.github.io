Refract

Your music, made visible.

Refract turns a song, or the soundtrack of a video you made, and a few photographs into a video that actually moves with the music — beats, drops and all — entirely on your iPhone.

Download on the App Store

What it does

Music from anywhere

Start from a song in Files, iCloud Drive or Voice Memos — or take the sound from a video you made, straight from Photos or Files, or from a direct link to an audio or video file. Refract checks the video first, keeps only its sound, lets you play it before you start, and can save it as an audio file of its own.

Light or dark

Refract follows your iPhone's Light or Dark appearance, or stays in the one you choose in Settings. The canvas and the timeline stay dark either way, so the colours you pick look the way they will in the video.

It listens first

Refract analyzes your track before it draws anything: tempo, every beat, where the drops are, and how the song divides into sections. That analysis runs on your device in a fraction of a second, and everything you see afterwards is built on it.

It choreographs the edit

Pick one of six looks and Refract builds the whole thing — visualizers that answer the frequencies, photo cuts landing on downbeats, effects that swell into the drop and settle after it. Not a filter running over your video; an edit composed from the structure of the song.

Then it hands you the timeline

A real multi-track editor underneath: eight visualizers, eight overlays, twenty effects, text, and per-clip timing you can drag. Every element has its own in and out points, and every parameter can be bound to the bass, the mids or the treble.

Ready to post

Export at 9:16, 4:5, 1:1 or 16:9, with presets for the places you actually post. What you see in the preview is exactly what renders, because both are the same function of the same timestamp.

Common questions

Can I use songs from Apple Music or Spotify?

No — those are DRM-protected and no app is allowed to read them. Refract works with music you own: an audio file from Files or shared in from another app, or the sound from a video you made. That restriction comes from Apple, and it applies to every app of this kind.

Does anything get uploaded?

No. Your music, your photographs and the videos you make never leave your iPhone. There is no account and no tracking — Refract only counts which features get used, anonymously and with no identifier. See the privacy policy.

Is it free?

Yes, with unlimited projects and exports, all four shapes, your own photos and the full timeline editor. The free tier includes a selection of the catalogue and exports up to 1080p at 30 fps, up to ten minutes, with a small Refract mark. Refract Pro opens the whole catalogue — every visualizer, overlay, effect and look, plus the Text tool — and adds 4K at 60 fps, any length, and no mark. The yearly plan starts with a free trial, and anything you have already made keeps working and keeps exporting either way.

What is a music visualizer app, and what does Refract make?

A music visualizer app turns sound into moving pictures. Refract makes a finished video: give it a song, or the sound from a video you made, plus a few photos, and it analyzes the track on your iPhone, finds the beats and the drops, and builds an edit where the visuals, the photo cuts and the effects follow the music. You can post the result as it is or open the timeline and change anything.

Can it make a video from a song and my photos?

Yes. That is the main thing it does. Pick one of six looks and Refract lays the photos on the downbeats, puts a visualizer behind them and times the effects to the song. There are eight visualizers, eight overlays, twenty effects and a text tool, and every parameter can react to the bass, the mids or the treble.

Can I use a link?

Yes, a direct link to an audio or video file. Refract downloads it, keeps the sound, lets you hear it before you start and can save it as an audio file of its own. Links to streaming pages are not files, so they do not work; see the first question about Apple Music and Spotify.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Analysis, editing and export all run on your iPhone. Only a link import needs a connection, to fetch the file.

Which sizes and formats does it export?

Vertical 9:16 for Reels, Shorts and TikTok, 4:5 and 1:1 for feeds, and 16:9 for YouTube, as an H.264 MP4. Free exports go up to 1080p at 30 fps and ten minutes; Refract Pro adds 4K at 60 fps and any length.

How is it different from STAELLA, Vibely or Vythm?

Those apps draw a live visualizer over the music, and some record the screen. Refract starts by analyzing the whole track and then composes a timed edit of your photos, visualizers and effects around its structure, with a real multi-track editor underneath. If you want a video of a track with your own pictures that lands on the beats, that is the difference. If you want a live light show while a song plays, they do that and Refract does not.

Which iPhones does it need?

Any iPhone on iOS 17 or later. Analysis and rendering run on the phone, so newer phones export faster, but nothing is locked to a model.
