Mimic

Privacy Policy

Effective 2026-09-10. Last updated 2026-09-11.

Your recordings stay on your iPhone. Recording, cloning and voice conversion all run on-device, and there is no account to create. The only thing that ever leaves your device is a voice you deliberately choose to publish to the Community Library — and even then Mimic sends a mathematical voice print, never your audio. Publishing attaches a random creator code so blocking works. If you never publish, nothing is ever sent.

What stays on your device

Unless you publish a voice (see below), everything Mimic captures or generates lives in the app's local storage on your iPhone. That includes:

  • Voice recordings — both the source audio you record into Mimic and any converted output saved in the Recordings tab. Audio is never uploaded, under any circumstances.
  • Voice avatars and cloned embeddings — when you clone a voice, Mimic extracts a small mathematical fingerprint (a 256-number speaker embedding) from your sample and stores it locally. The original recording stays local too; you can delete either at any time.
  • Voice conversion — both live (Performer Mode) and offline conversion run on-device through Core ML on Apple's Neural Engine. No audio is uploaded for processing.
  • Pro entitlement — your purchase status is stored locally and verified through Apple's StoreKit. Mimic does not maintain its own user account system.

The Community Library

Mimic 1.3 added a library of voices people have chosen to share. Using it is optional, and browsing it sends us nothing about you.

If you publish a voice

Publishing is deliberate: you pick a voice you created, write a short description, and confirm on a separate screen that you have permission to share it. When you do, Mimic sends to our server:

  • the voice's 256-number speaker embedding — a mathematical signature, not audio, and not reversible into your recording;
  • the name and description you typed;
  • the thumbnail image attached to that voice, if it has one.

Your recordings are never sent. There is no way for another user to hear your actual voice sample.

A published voice is public: anyone using Mimic can see its name, description and thumbnail, and can add it to their own app. Please do not put personal information in the name or description, and do not use a photo you would not want to be public.

Moderation and takedown

  • Nothing appears automatically. Every submission is reviewed by hand before it becomes visible to anyone. A submission that is not approved is never shown.
  • Anyone can report a voice from inside the app, with a reason. Reports reach us immediately.
  • Reported voices are hidden automatically once enough reports arrive, without waiting for us to read them, and stay hidden pending review.
  • You can remove your voice at any time from the app. It disappears from the library; copies other people already added to their own device remain on their device, as we have no way to reach into it.
  • To ask about a published voice, or to report one outside the app, email the address at the bottom of this page.

What the server keeps

The server stores approved voices and, for each, a count of how many people liked and downloaded it. It keeps reports so we can act on them. It records the IP address of publish and report requests for a short period, purely to rate-limit abuse. There is no account and no profile, and the like and download counts are plain totals, not records of who did what.

The publisher tag

Publishing to the Community Library is entirely optional, and the library works the way any shared space does: a voice has a creator. When you publish your first voice, Mimic creates a random code and stores it on your device, so the library can tell that several voices came from the same creator.

That is what makes blocking work. Block a creator and every voice of theirs disappears from your library, including whatever they publish next — without the code, blocking could only hide one voice at a time. It also keeps any one person from flooding the review queue.

  • It is random — not derived from your device, your Apple Account, your name or anything about you, and not Apple's advertising identifier.
  • It is only created if you publish. If you never publish a voice, one is never made.
  • We never store the code itself — only a one-way hash of it. Other people see a short fragment of that hash and nothing more.
  • It is not used for advertising, analytics or tracking, and it is never shared with anyone else.
  • Nobody can look you up by it. It is never shown as a name, and it links to nothing but the voices you chose to publish.

Because the code persists, Mimic's App Store privacy label lists Identifiers alongside User Content. It is used for app functionality and nothing else.

Blocking

You can block a creator from inside the app by pressing and holding any voice in the Community Library. Every voice from that creator disappears from your library, including anything they publish afterwards. Blocking happens entirely on your device — we are not told about it — and you can undo it any time in Settings → Blocked Creators.

Permissions Mimic may request

Mimic asks for permissions only at the moment you opt into a feature that needs them. You can revoke any of them in iOS Settings at any time.

  • Microphone — to record your voice in Performer Mode and for cloning.
  • Camera and Photo Library — only if you choose to attach a picture to one of your voices. Photos you pick stay on your device unless you publish that voice, in which case the picture becomes its public thumbnail.
  • Notifications — when granted, Mimic schedules local reminders such as a heads-up before your Pro trial ends. No remote push servers are used.

That is the entire permission surface. Mimic does not request Contacts, Location, Calendar, HealthKit, Speech Recognition, or any other capability.

What Mimic does not do

  • We do not upload your audio. Recordings and voice samples never leave your device.
  • We do not run analytics, and there is no telemetry about how you use the app.
  • We do not include third-party SDKs for advertising, tracking, attribution, crash reporting, or social media.
  • We do not require an account. Publishing a voice attaches a random creator code so blocking works — see The publisher tag. It is not used for advertising, analytics or tracking.
  • We do not sell, share or rent your data.
  • We do not track you across other companies' apps and websites.

On-device machine learning

Mimic's voice cloning uses a Core ML adaptation of the open-source OpenVoice v2 model. Both the speaker-embedding extractor and the voice converter run locally on your iPhone's Neural Engine. Inference never touches the network, and no inference output leaves the device unless you explicitly share it through iOS's share sheet or publish the voice.

The first launch may download additional Apple-provided audio frameworks the OS uses on-device; these are managed by iOS and not by Mimic.

Sharing what you create

When you tap the share button on a converted recording, iOS opens its standard share sheet. The audio file is handed off only to the destination you pick (Messages, Mail, AirDrop, Files, etc.). Mimic itself does not see or relay the content of the share.

Purchases

Mimic Pro is sold through Apple's StoreKit as either a monthly subscription, which starts with a 7-day free trial, or a one-time lifetime purchase. Apple processes the transaction; Mimic receives only the entitlement status and never sees your payment details. To cancel a subscription, use the Subscriptions section of your Apple ID in iOS Settings.

Acceptable use

Mimic is intended for personal creative use — performing, voicing characters, narration and dubbing for your own videos, accessibility, and similar. Using Mimic to impersonate someone without their consent, commit fraud, harass another person, or produce non-consensual sexual content is prohibited by Mimic's terms and likely illegal in your jurisdiction. Publishing a voice that is not yours to share is prohibited, and published voices that break these rules are removed.

Children's privacy

Mimic is not directed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. The Community Library is moderated before anything becomes visible, and Mimic has no accounts, profiles, comments or messaging, so users cannot contact one another. If you believe a child has published personal information through Mimic, email us and we will remove it.

Changes to this policy

If Mimic changes how it handles data, we will update this page, bump the effective date above, and surface the change inside the app before it takes effect.
